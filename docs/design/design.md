# Design

Companion to `docs/specs/spec.md`. Status: **first slice built**. The decisions below are what the first playable board does; everything under *Questions to resolve here* is still open.

Direction from the brief, to be developed here:

- Playing should feel like sitting across from a person, not using a website.
- Cards as physical objects: hover and drag are visible to the opponent in real time.
- Simple art for now: lands that read as simplified MTG basics, abstract MTG-style card backs.
- Expressive effect animations, e.g. a Mountain becoming a meteor that crushes the targeted land.
- Performance is a feature. Deep modularity so features can be added or replaced without touching the core.
- Reference points: the webOS project (native-program feel, modularity) and Hearthstone (physicality of cards).

Presence signals (hover, drag, cursor) are cosmetic. They travel on their own throwaway channel and never touch game state. Technically, see the ephemeral channel in `docs/tech/tech.md`.

## Decided so far

- **Rendering:** the table is a PixiJS (WebGL) canvas; everything around it (lobby, invite codes, status line, buttons) is plain HTML. HTML gives text input, copy-paste and screen readers for free; WebGL is where card tilt, glow and particle effects will live. Board state and input logic stay separate from drawing, so the renderer can change without touching the rest.
- **Layout (from the viewer's seat):** five rows top to bottom: their hand, their battlefield, the middle (the land being played sits here during a counter chain), your battlefield, your hand. Graveyards are small fanned piles left of each battlefield; decks are piles on the right with a count.
- **Cards glide between zones.** Every card is one persistent sprite that eases toward its new position, so plays, counters and destroys read as movement before any bespoke animation exists.
- **Click or drag.** Playable hand cards glow gold. Clicking one plays it, or, if it needs a declared target, lifts it and makes the valid targets glow red; click a target to play, anything else to cancel. The same cards can be dragged: the card follows the pointer, and a card with several targets lights its targets as it lifts. Drop it on a target to play it there, anywhere above your hand row to play it (or, with several targets, to leave it picked for a click), or back on your hand to put it back. A press only becomes a drag after 6 px of travel, so a slightly shaky click still clicks. Counter-chain choices (allow, or each Island + other pair) and Pass are buttons in the status bar. Resign asks for confirmation.
- **Phones and touch.** Below 720 px wide (a phone held upright) the table switches to a compact layout: cards are sized by row height rather than width (about twice the desktop-scaled size on a 390 px phone), each hand overlaps across the full width with only the deck pile beside it, each battlefield sits beside a slim graveyard column, and the status bar keeps room for a two-line prompt so the table does not jump as prompts change. Tap plays like a click. A finger drag needs 12 px of travel (fingers wobble), and the dragged card is held above the fingertip so the finger does not hide it; the drop lands where the card is drawn. A phone held sideways uses the desktop layout, limited by its height: playable, with small cards. The canvas takes no browser gestures (`touch-action: none`), and long-press callouts and menus are suppressed on it.
- **Card info.** What a face-up land does (its "Effect when played" text from the spec; Island also says how it counters) appears in a small panel above the card: after resting the mouse on it for 0.3 s, or while a finger long-presses it (0.45 s, with a short vibration where supported). Lifting the finger or moving off the card hides it; a long press never plays the card, and turning it into a drag hides the panel. Card backs show nothing. The panel is local only and sends nothing to the opponent.
- **History.** A 📜 button in the status bar opens a panel listing the match so far, turn by turn ("Turn 12 · yours"), in plain words: "They played Mountain, aiming at your Island.", "You countered with Island + Forest.", "Their Mountain was countered.", "Your Swamp made them discard Mountain.", ending with the result. A coloured edge marks who acted (blue you, red them); outcomes nobody chose are muted. It follows new lines while scrolled to the end, and stays put while you read back. It never says more than you may know: the opponent's draws are "They drew a card", and your own are named once your board knows the card. On desktop it sits at the top right of the table; on a phone it spans the width but stops short of your hand, so you can keep playing under it. Escape or ✕ closes it.
- **Whose move it is.** A soft band of light runs along the edge of whoever the game is waiting on: gold along your side (breathing gently) when it is you, salmon along theirs when it is them. It follows the actual decision, not just the turn: in a counter window it is on the responder's side, while choosing an effect's target on the controller's. A badge left of the middle row shows the turn number and whose turn it is ("Turn 7 · Your turn"), or "Game over".
- **Targeting arrow.** A card that needs a target does not follow the pointer when dragged: it stays lifted in your hand and a dashed arrow, bowing upward, runs from it to your pointer or finger. Over a valid target the arrow turns solid red and rings the target; release there to play. The same arrow follows the mouse after click-picking such a card. The opponent sees it too: their aiming card lifts and the arrow points where they point, ringing whatever public card is under it (their legal targets are not ours to know). A card with one way to be played still drags as before.
- **Tilt and shine.** A card under the mouse grows slightly, leans toward the pointer and catches a light streak that slides across its face as the pointer moves. Mouse only: on touch, a long press shows card info instead.
- **Forced moves are invisible.** Granting draw keys and revealing a hand to Swamp happen automatically.
- **Hidden information:** opponent hand cards are backs until revealed; a revealed card stays face-up in their hand, marked with a pale rim and an eye badge.
- **Effect animations** (`src/board/fx.ts`), one per land effect, layered on top of the card glide:
  - Mountain: a meteor falls on the target with an ember trail; on impact a shockwave ring, debris, a screen shake, and only then does the land leave for the graveyard.
  - Island counter: the paying cards splash and hold a beat, then a bolt of water arcs from each into the land on the stack, which takes the hit (a flash, ripples, spray, a small shake). A countered land cracks across its face, then bursts into spinning blue shards on its way to the graveyard.
  - Land entering play: a thump (scale bump) and a puff of dust.
  - Forest: a green glow in the graveyard, then a vine grows along the path the returning card will take to its owner's hand, sprouting leaves; leaves burst where it lands.
  - Swamp: the reveal is a moment of its own. While the Swamp's controller picks the discard, the revealed hand fans out large and slightly tilted in the middle of the table over a darkened backdrop, labelled "Their hand, revealed: choose one to discard" (or "Your hand, revealed: they choose one to discard"); each card flashes as it arrives, one after another. The Swamp waits in its battlefield spot. The chosen card is grabbed by dark tendrils and darkens, then bursts into smoke; the rest glide back to the hand. A Plains reusing a Swamp gets the same moment.
  - Plains: a golden halo around the land it reuses.
  - Win: a gold fountain and a Victory/Defeat banner.
  Effects only decorate: every card still ends where the layout puts it, so an interrupted effect can never leave the view wrong. With `prefers-reduced-motion`, effects are skipped and only the glide remains.
- **Presence** (`src/board/presence.ts`): the card under the opponent's pointer gets a cyan glow and leans toward the middle of the table, and their pointer shows as a cyan dot (flipped to your side, fading after 2 s idle). Hands keep a local visual order that shuffles each new card in, and a hidden hand card is sent only as its index among the sender's hidden cards in that order, so the opponent sees which card you touch but never which slot it is. A drag travels the same way: the opponent sees that card (a back, if hidden) pulled out of your hand and following your pointer, smoothed between ~20 Hz updates; if the updates stop for 0.6 s (the end message is lost on the unreliable channel) it drops back into place. Known quirk: when a hand mixes revealed and hidden cards, the two views may interleave them differently, so the lifted back can sit a position away from the cursor.
- **Where effects come from:** the engine returns states, not events, so the board compares consecutive states (`src/board/effects.ts`): each card that changed zone becomes a cue, labelled by the pending play that caused it. This keeps the engine untouched; if engine events are added later, cues can come from them instead.
- **Counter chain:** while a play waits on the stack, each counter's two paid cards sit beside it rather than in the graveyard, alternating right and left of the play (first counter on the right) and leaning toward the payer's side, labelled "1. Your counter", "2. Their counter" and so on. Under the play, "Resolves if allowed" or "Countered if allowed" says what the parity means right now. When the chain ends, the paid cards glide to their graveyards. The board rebuilds the chain from the action log since the last play (`src/board/chain.ts`), so it survives a tab reload; if the log ever disagrees with the state, the cards simply show in their graveyards.
- **Reconnecting online:** if the link drops, a panel over the table asks both players to swap fresh codes (either may make one), and play pauses until it is back; a brief network wobble shows a waiting message first. A reloaded tab returns to the lobby with "Your online match is still going", the same code swap, and an "Abandon it" option. Two tabs on one computer need no codes: reloading and retaking the seat resumes.
- **Card art** (`src/board/art.ts`), drawn in code and baked into one texture per design when the table mounts, so the table costs no more to draw than flat rectangles: each basic land has an MTG-style layout (name bar, a small landscape, "Basic Land" type line, a large mana symbol in the text box). Plains: sun over fields; Island: islet and waves; Swamp: moon, dead tree, bog; Mountain: snow-capped peaks; Forest: layered pines. The card back is a leather-brown oval carrying the five land colours in a ring. A hand card the opponent has seen gets a pale rim and an eye badge in the corner.

## Resolved

- **Board layout**: the five-row layout above, with deck counts, graveyards, and the turn badge and edge light for whose move it is.
- **Interaction model**: targets light red *and* a targeting arrow is drawn; lighting alone was not enough to read at a glance, especially for the opponent.
- **Hidden information**: revealed hand cards stay marked (rim and eye badge); a Swamp reveal gets its own moment with the hand fanned in the middle.
- **WebAssembly**: no, for now. The engine is cheap, setup crypto is fast with the 256-bit modulus, and rendering is PixiJS. Revisit only if the modulus grows a lot.
- **Tilt effects**: done as a code-only tilt and light streak on hover.

## Questions to resolve here

- Card art beyond the code-drawn first pass: real illustrations (commissioned, generated, or richer code-drawn scenes), foil.
